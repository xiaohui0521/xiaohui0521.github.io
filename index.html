About

I am a PhD researcher in Psychology at the University of Edinburgh. My research lies at the intersection of personality, individual differences, and close relationships, with a broader interest in understanding how personal characteristics and social contexts shape well-being and behaviour.

My work examines questions related to romantic relationships, happiness, inequality, perceived scarcity, and digital behaviour. Methodologically, I work with longitudinal, diary, cross-cultural, and large-scale survey data.

Research

I am interested in how psychological processes unfold across different social and cultural contexts, and how individual experiences interact with broader economic and societal conditions. My previous work has examined smartphone addiction and aggression, moral emotions, marriage and happiness, social inequality, narcissism, belonging, and perceived scarcity.
